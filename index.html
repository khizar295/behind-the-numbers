Helpful tax insights

Tax questions, explained clearly

Straightforward guidance on common accounting and tax questions for New Zealand business owners.

Business expenses

Which business expenses can I claim?

In general, a business may be able to claim expenses that are genuinely connected with earning its income. Common examples can include business-use software, office costs, professional fees, advertising and work-related travel.

Private or personal costs are not automatically deductible. If something is used for both business and personal purposes, you may need to identify and claim only the business portion.

Keep invoices, receipts and a clear record of the business purpose. The correct treatment depends on the facts and the applicable Inland Revenue rules.

GST

When does my business need to register for GST?

In New Zealand, GST registration is generally required when your taxable activity meets the registration threshold set by Inland Revenue, or when you expect it to reach that threshold in the relevant period.

You can also choose to register voluntarily in some circumstances. Before deciding, consider how GST will affect your prices, invoices, returns, record keeping and cash flow.

The threshold and your circumstances matter, so check the current Inland Revenue requirements before registering or deciding that registration is not needed.

Learn more Explore GST registration guidance
Business structure

Should I operate as a sole trader or a company?

A sole trader structure can be simpler to establish and administer. The individual carries on the business personally and is generally responsible for its business obligations.

A company is a separate legal entity. It can provide a different ownership and governance structure, but it also brings additional administration, reporting and compliance responsibilities.

The right option depends on your business plans, risk, finances and circumstances. It is worth getting advice before choosing or changing your structure.
