How we can help

A clearer view of where your business is heading

Running a business involves making decisions with incomplete information and changing circumstances. Management reporting can help bring your financial information together so you can see what is happening and consider what may need attention.

We provide practical support that can be tailored to your business, from reviewing regular results to preparing budgets and forecasts. The aim is to make financial information easier to understand and more useful in day-to-day management.

Whether you are looking to improve visibility over costs, understand your margins or plan for a new stage of growth, a structured approach to your numbers can help you ask better questions and make decisions with greater clarity.

A practical management tool

What can regular reporting help you see?

A set of financial reports is most useful when it helps answer real business questions. Depending on your reporting needs, reviewing results regularly can help you spot patterns, investigate unexpected movements and decide where to focus attention.

Comparing current results with previous periods or your budget can provide context for changes in revenue, expenses and profitability. It can also help you identify areas where further investigation or a change in approach may be worth considering.

Reports do not remove uncertainty or guarantee future results. They provide a structured view of available information to support better-informed conversations and decisions.

Revenue trends

Are sales moving in line with expectations? Are there seasonal patterns or changes in customer activity?

Margins and costs

Are costs changing, and how are your gross and net margins responding?

Cash position

What cash may be needed for upcoming commitments and operating expenses?

Progress against plan

Are actual results tracking against your budget and business objectives?

Making sense of your numbers

Financial information that tells a bigger story

Individual figures are useful, but looking at them together can provide a more complete picture of your business. Different reports help answer different questions.

Profit and loss

A profit and loss report summarises income and expenses over a selected period. It can help you understand how revenue, operating costs and profitability have changed.

  • Sales and other income
  • Direct costs and overheads
  • Gross and net profit

Balance sheet

A balance sheet provides a snapshot of the business's assets, liabilities and equity at a particular date. It helps show the financial position of the business.

  • Business assets
  • Outstanding liabilities
  • Owner's equity

Cash-flow report

Cash-flow information helps you understand money moving into and out of the business, including the timing of receipts and payments.

  • Customer payments
  • Supplier and operating costs
  • Upcoming cash commitments

Looking ahead

Budgeting and forecasting for better preparation

A budget gives your business a financial plan to work towards. It brings together expected revenue, operating expenses, planned investments and other financial commitments over a chosen period.

Forecasting takes this a step further by allowing you to update your expectations as new information becomes available. This can be particularly useful when sales patterns, costs or business priorities change.

Rather than treating a budget as a fixed document, regular reviews can help you understand where your business stands and whether your plans need to be adjusted.

Frequently asked questions

Some common questions about business advisory and management reporting.

What are management accounts?

Management accounts are internal financial reports prepared to help business owners and managers monitor performance. They may include profit and loss information, balance sheet details, cash-flow information and selected performance measures.

How often should my business review its numbers?

The right frequency depends on the size, complexity and pace of your business. Some businesses benefit from monthly reporting, while others may need a different schedule. The important point is to have information available often enough to support timely decisions.

Do I need a budget if my business is small?

A simple budget can be useful for a small business too. It can help you estimate regular costs, plan for quieter periods and think about the sales needed to cover expenses. It should be proportionate to your business and practical to maintain.

Can forecasting predict exactly what will happen?

No. A forecast is an estimate based on assumptions and information available at the time. Its value is in helping you explore possible outcomes, identify cash requirements and update plans when actual results or circumstances change.

Can advisory support be tailored to my business?

Yes. The reporting and advisory work should reflect your business goals, available records and the questions you need answered. We can discuss your requirements and agree an appropriate scope.

What information is needed to prepare management reports?

The information required depends on the type of reporting involved. It may include accounting records, bank information, sales and expense details, payroll information, existing budgets and other relevant business records. We can discuss what is needed based on your requirements.

What is the difference between profit and cash flow?

Profit measures income earned less expenses incurred over a period. Cash flow looks at the actual movement of money into and out of the business. A business can be profitable while still experiencing cash-flow pressure because customer payments and business expenses may occur at different times.

Do you provide one-off or ongoing advisory support?

The scope can depend on your business needs. You may need help with a particular planning exercise or prefer regular reporting and review discussions. We can talk through your requirements and agree an appropriate arrangement.
