Which Business Expenses Can I Claim?

Understanding which business costs may be tax-deductible can help you manage your finances, maintain accurate records and avoid unnecessary tax complications.

New Zealand Business Guide Accounting Insights Practical Guide

Understanding Business Expenses

Running a business involves a wide range of costs. These can include everyday office supplies, rent, professional fees, employee expenses, technology, advertising and travel. Some of these costs may be deductible when calculating your taxable business income.

In general, a business expense needs to have a genuine connection with earning your business income. The expense should be incurred for business purposes, and you should be able to provide appropriate supporting records if required.

However, not every payment made through a business bank account automatically becomes a tax deduction. Personal purchases, private costs and certain capital expenses may need different treatment.

The general rule

A business expense should relate to earning business income. If a cost has both business and private purposes, you generally need to identify the business portion and apply the relevant tax rules.

Common Business Expenses You May Be Able to Claim

The types of expenses your business can claim depend on the work you do, how the costs are used and the applicable Inland Revenue requirements. Here are some common categories to consider.

Office and Premises Costs

Business rent, electricity, water, office supplies, cleaning and other eligible costs associated with operating your workplace.

Software and Technology

Business software subscriptions, cloud services, accounting platforms, eligible online tools and technology expenses.

Advertising and Marketing

Eligible advertising campaigns, website costs, promotional materials, business photography and marketing services.

Professional Fees

Qualifying accounting, bookkeeping, legal and other professional services used in operating your business.

Vehicle and Travel Costs

Eligible business-related vehicle use, business travel, accommodation, parking and transport costs.

Staff and Employment Costs

Qualifying wages, salaries, employer contributions, recruitment costs and other eligible staff-related expenses.

Business Insurance

Eligible premiums for business-related insurance, such as public liability, professional indemnity and business asset cover.

Stock and Supplies

Business-related materials, supplies, packaging and trading stock, subject to the relevant accounting and tax treatment.

These are examples rather than automatic deductions. Eligibility, timing, apportionment and documentation requirements can differ depending on the expense.

Can I Claim Home Office Expenses?

If you operate your business from home, you may be able to claim a portion of certain household expenses. This can be relevant for sole traders, consultants, contractors and business owners who use part of their home for business activities.

Depending on your circumstances, eligible costs may include a proportion of household expenses such as electricity, internet, rates, rent or mortgage interest. The applicable method and requirements depend on the type of property, business use and current Inland Revenue guidance.

What should you consider?

  • Whether a specific area of your home is used for business purposes.
  • How much of the property is used for business activities.
  • How frequently and for what purpose the space is used.
  • Whether you have the supporting information needed to calculate the business-use portion.

Example: Working from home

A self-employed consultant uses a dedicated room in their home as an office. They may be entitled to claim an appropriate business-use portion of eligible household expenses, provided the relevant requirements are met.

What If an Expense Is Partly Personal?

Many business owners use the same resources for both business and personal activities. Common examples include mobile phones, internet connections, vehicles and home offices.

When an expense has mixed business and private use, you generally need to identify the business portion and apply the relevant tax rules. Claiming the entire amount may not be appropriate if part of the cost relates to private use.

Example: Business and personal phone use

If you use your mobile phone for both business and personal calls, you may need to work out a reasonable business-use proportion. Your records should support how you arrived at the amount claimed.

The same principle can apply to vehicles, internet services and other shared resources. Keeping a reasonable record of business use can help support the treatment of these costs.

Understanding Business Vehicle Expenses

If you use a vehicle for business purposes, some related costs may be deductible. However, the amount you can claim generally depends on how much the vehicle is used for business compared with private travel.

Potentially relevant vehicle costs include:

Fuel and charging costs
Repairs and maintenance
Vehicle insurance
Eligible registration and road costs

Depending on your circumstances, you may need to maintain a logbook or use another permitted method to establish the business-use proportion. Different rules may apply to different vehicle ownership and business arrangements.

It is important to distinguish business travel from ordinary private travel, including travel between home and a regular workplace where applicable.

Revenue Expenses and Capital Expenses

Not every business purchase is treated in the same way for tax purposes. One important distinction is whether a cost is a day-to-day operating expense or a capital purchase.

Revenue Expenses

These are generally the ongoing costs involved in operating a business and earning income.

  • Office supplies
  • Routine software subscriptions
  • Business rent
  • Eligible professional fees

Capital Expenses

These generally relate to acquiring or improving assets that provide longer-term value to the business.

  • Machinery and equipment
  • Business vehicles
  • Computers and larger assets
  • Certain property improvements

Capital assets may be subject to depreciation or other specific tax rules rather than being deducted in the same way as everyday operating expenses. The correct treatment depends on the nature of the asset and the applicable rules.

Business Expenses and GST

Income tax deductions and GST claims are related but are not the same thing. A cost may have different treatment for income tax and GST purposes, depending on your GST registration status and the nature of the purchase.

If your business is GST-registered, you may be able to claim GST input tax deductions on eligible business purchases, provided the relevant requirements are satisfied.

When reviewing GST on business expenses, consider:

  • Whether your business is registered for GST.
  • Whether the purchase relates to your taxable business activity.
  • Whether you have the required taxable supply information or supporting records.
  • Whether any private or exempt use needs to be accounted for.

Important distinction

Being able to claim an expense for income tax does not automatically mean you can claim all the GST associated with it. GST treatment should be checked separately.

What About Business Entertainment Expenses?

Business entertainment can include meals, events, hospitality and other activities involving clients, suppliers or employees. Some entertainment expenses may be fully deductible, while others may be subject to specific restrictions.

In New Zealand, certain entertainment expenses are generally only 50% deductible for income tax purposes. However, the rules depend on the type of entertainment and the circumstances.

Examples that may require special consideration include:

  • Meals and hospitality provided to clients or business associates.
  • Certain tickets to sporting, cultural or entertainment events.
  • Certain staff functions and business celebrations.
  • Some gifts and hospitality provided as part of business relationships.

Entertainment expenses can have specific income tax and GST implications. It is worth checking the rules before recording or claiming these costs.

Why Keeping Accurate Business Records Matters

Good record keeping is an essential part of managing a business. It helps you support expense claims, prepare financial statements, monitor cash flow and complete tax returns accurately.

Keeping your records organised throughout the year can also reduce the time and effort involved in preparing your accounts at year-end.

Business Expense Record-Keeping Checklist

  • Keep invoices, receipts and supporting documents for business purchases.
  • Record the business purpose of expenses where it may not be obvious.
  • Separate business transactions from personal spending as far as practical.
  • Maintain vehicle logbook or usage information where required.
  • Keep records of home office calculations and business-use proportions.
  • Reconcile bank transactions regularly using accounting software.
  • Retain records for the required period.

New Zealand businesses generally need to keep tax records for at least seven years. Some situations may have additional requirements, so it is important to check the relevant Inland Revenue guidance.

How Accounting Software Can Help

Using accounting software can make it easier to organise business expenses and maintain up-to-date financial records. Rather than relying entirely on spreadsheets or paper receipts, you can keep many of your transactions together in one place.

Depending on the software and setup, useful features may include:

Expense Categorisation

Organise transactions into relevant expense categories for easier reporting.

Receipt Management

Store digital copies of supporting documents alongside relevant transactions.

Bank Reconciliation

Match recorded transactions with bank activity and identify items that need attention.

Financial Reporting

Review business costs and understand how expenses affect your financial position.

Accounting software does not replace professional judgement. Transactions still need to be reviewed and coded correctly, particularly where an expense has private use, capital treatment or special tax rules.

Common Business Expense Mistakes to Avoid

Incorrect expense treatment can create problems when preparing financial statements or tax returns. These are some common mistakes business owners should watch out for.

Claiming Personal Purchases

A personal purchase does not become a business deduction simply because it was paid from a business account.

Claiming the Full Cost of Mixed-Use Items

Costs used for both business and personal purposes generally need to be apportioned appropriately.

Not Keeping Supporting Documents

Missing receipts and invoices can make it harder to support the treatment of business expenses.

Treating Every Purchase as an Operating Expense

Capital purchases may need to be recorded and treated differently from everyday operating costs.

Overlooking Special Tax Rules

Certain entertainment, vehicle and other expenses may have specific income tax or GST requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Here are answers to some common questions about claiming business expenses in New Zealand.

Can I claim every expense I pay for through my business account?

No. Paying for something through a business account does not automatically make it tax-deductible. The expense generally needs to relate to earning business income and satisfy the applicable tax requirements. Personal expenses should be treated separately.

Can I claim expenses if I work from home?

You may be able to claim an appropriate business-use portion of certain household expenses if you operate your business from home. The amount and method depend on your circumstances and Inland Revenue requirements.

How long should I keep business expense records?

New Zealand businesses generally need to keep tax records for at least seven years. Keep invoices, receipts, bank records and other documents that support your business transactions.

Can I claim vehicle expenses if I use my car privately as well?

You may be able to claim the business-use portion of eligible vehicle costs. You need to use an appropriate method to establish business use and retain supporting information.

Are capital purchases treated as normal business expenses?

Not necessarily. Capital assets, such as machinery, vehicles and certain equipment, may be subject to depreciation or other specific tax rules rather than being deducted in the same way as everyday operating expenses.

Can I claim GST on all my business expenses?

No. GST input tax deductions depend on your GST registration status, the nature of the purchase and whether it relates to your taxable business activity. Supporting information and specific GST rules also apply.

Are business entertainment expenses fully deductible?

Some entertainment expenses are subject to specific restrictions. Certain expenses are generally only 50% deductible for income tax purposes, although the treatment depends on the type of entertainment and the circumstances.

Should I speak with an accountant before claiming an unusual expense?

It can be helpful to seek advice when an expense has mixed business and personal use, relates to a capital asset, involves entertainment or has specific GST implications. An accountant can help you understand the appropriate treatment.
