When Does My Business Need to Register for GST?

Understanding New Zealand's GST registration requirements can help you stay compliant, manage your cash flow and avoid unexpected tax obligations as your business grows.

New Zealand business guide GST registration and returns

Understanding GST in New Zealand

The basics every business owner should know

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a tax on most goods and services supplied in New Zealand. It is collected by businesses on behalf of Inland Revenue and is generally included in the price customers pay for taxable goods and services.

Businesses registered for GST generally charge GST on their taxable sales and may be able to claim GST back on eligible business purchases and expenses. The difference between the GST collected and the GST paid is accounted for through GST returns.

The standard GST rate in New Zealand is 15%. GST is separate from income tax, so registering for GST does not replace your normal income tax responsibilities.

New Zealand GST registration threshold

$60,000

You generally need to register if your taxable activity has turnover of at least $60,000 in the last 12 months, or you expect it to reach at least $60,000 in the next 12 months.

Look backwards

Check whether your taxable turnover reached the threshold during the previous 12 months.

Look forwards

Consider whether you reasonably expect your taxable turnover to reach the threshold in the next 12 months.

When Are You Required to Register for GST?

In New Zealand, GST registration is generally compulsory when either of the following situations applies to your taxable activity:

Your turnover has reached the threshold

Your taxable supplies were at least $60,000 over the previous 12-month period.

You expect to reach the threshold

You reasonably expect your taxable supplies to be at least $60,000 over the next 12 months.

You may also be required to register if you carry out a taxable activity and start adding GST to the prices of your goods or services, even if your turnover is below the threshold.

Important to remember

The registration threshold is based on taxable turnover, not your business profit. A business can have a low profit or even make a loss while still meeting the GST registration threshold.

What Counts Towards the $60,000 Threshold?

The GST threshold is based on the value of taxable supplies made as part of your taxable activity. It is not simply the amount of money left in your business after paying expenses.

For example, if you operate a consulting business, the fees you earn from providing taxable consulting services will generally contribute towards your taxable turnover.

Common taxable activities

  • Providing professional services
  • Selling products and merchandise
  • Charging for business services

What to consider

  • Sales from your taxable activity
  • Expected future business sales
  • Whether supplies are taxable or exempt

Some supplies have different GST treatment, including zero-rated and exempt supplies. These should not be treated interchangeably. If your business has different types of income, it is worth getting advice on how they affect your registration position.

Does Every New Business Need to Register?

No. Starting a business does not automatically mean you must register for GST. If your taxable turnover is below the threshold and you do not otherwise meet a compulsory registration requirement, registration may not be necessary.

For example, a small business that is just starting out and earning modest revenue may be able to operate without GST registration initially.

However, this does not mean you can ignore GST completely. Your business activity and turnover should be monitored as your sales increase.

Signs your business should review its GST position

Your monthly sales have started increasing consistently.

You have secured a large contract or new customer.

Your business expects significant growth over the next year.

Your taxable turnover is approaching $60,000.

Can I Register for GST Voluntarily?

Yes. Some businesses choose to register even when their turnover is below $60,000. This is known as voluntary GST registration.

Voluntary registration may be worth considering if your business has significant eligible expenses or deals mainly with other GST-registered businesses.

Potential advantages

  • • Claim GST on eligible business purchases.
  • • Work within GST processes from an early stage.
  • • May suit businesses with GST-registered customers.

Potential disadvantages

  • • Additional administration and record keeping.
  • • GST returns must be filed.
  • • GST may affect customer pricing and cash flow.

Before registering voluntarily, consider your customers, pricing, expenses, cash flow and the ongoing administration involved. Registering is not automatically beneficial for every small business.

A Simple Example: When Should You Register?

Imagine you run a small consulting business in Auckland. Your taxable sales have been growing steadily, and you expect to take on more clients over the coming months.

Illustrative business example

Expected taxable sales over the next 12 months

$72,000

GST registration threshold

$60,000

What does this mean?

If the business reasonably expects its taxable supplies to reach at least $60,000 in the next 12 months, it generally needs to register for GST. The owner should review the timing of registration and take the necessary steps with Inland Revenue.

This is why it is important to look ahead rather than waiting until the end of the financial year. A business can meet the registration requirement based on its reasonable expectations about future turnover.

What Happens After GST Registration?

Once registered, your business needs to manage its GST responsibilities correctly. These commonly include:

Charge GST

Apply the correct GST treatment to taxable sales and provide the required taxable supply information.

Track GST

Keep accurate records of GST collected on sales and GST paid on eligible business purchases.

File GST returns

Submit GST returns according to the filing frequency assigned or selected for your business.

Manage payments

Pay any GST due by the relevant due date or receive a refund where applicable.

Choosing Your GST Filing Frequency

When registering, you will need to consider the taxable period that suits your business. GST returns may generally be filed monthly, two-monthly or six-monthly, depending on eligibility and the options available.

Monthly filing

Returns are prepared and submitted every month. This may suit businesses with higher transaction volumes or specific reporting needs.

Two-monthly filing

Returns are submitted every two months. This is a common option for many businesses.

Six-monthly filing

Eligible businesses may be able to file every six months. This can reduce filing frequency but requires careful planning for GST payments.

The right filing frequency depends on factors such as transaction volume, cash flow, business size and how frequently you want to manage your GST obligations. Check current Inland Revenue requirements when selecting your filing frequency.

How GST Can Affect Your Business Cash Flow

GST is not business income. When you collect GST from customers, that amount may need to be paid to Inland Revenue after accounting for eligible GST credits.

If GST collected is mixed with everyday business funds, it can be easy to spend money that will later be required for a GST payment. This can create unnecessary pressure when a return is due.

Set money aside

Keep track of GST collected and plan ahead for upcoming payments.

Review your position

Regularly review GST reports so you know what may be payable or refundable.

Good cash-flow planning helps prevent surprises and gives business owners a clearer picture of the money available for operating expenses.

Why Accurate Bookkeeping Is Important

Good bookkeeping makes GST management easier. If sales, purchases and bank transactions are not recorded correctly, it can become difficult to prepare accurate GST returns.

Your accounting records should provide a clear picture of business income, expenses, GST collected and GST paid. Keeping these records organised throughout the year can also make it easier to identify errors before filing deadlines.

A practical GST bookkeeping checklist

Reconcile business bank accounts regularly.
Keep invoices and receipts for business transactions.
Record sales and purchases accurately.
Use accounting software to organise records.
Review GST reports before submitting returns.
Keep track of GST filing and payment dates.

Common GST Mistakes Small Businesses Should Avoid

GST responsibilities can become difficult to manage when records are incomplete or registration requirements are overlooked. Being aware of common mistakes can help you avoid unnecessary complications.

Waiting too long to register

Failing to monitor turnover can mean you miss the point at which registration becomes compulsory.

Keeping incomplete records

Missing invoices and unrecorded transactions can make GST calculations less reliable.

Mixing GST with available cash

Spending GST collected from customers without planning for the eventual payment can create cash-flow problems.

Missing filing deadlines

Not keeping track of return and payment dates can lead to late filing and possible penalties or interest.

Claiming ineligible expenses

Not every purchase qualifies for a GST claim. Make sure claims are supported by appropriate records and meet the relevant requirements.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About GST

Here are some common questions New Zealand business owners ask about GST registration and ongoing responsibilities.

Do I need to register for GST if I earn less than $60,000?

Not necessarily. If your taxable turnover is below $60,000 over the relevant 12-month periods and you do not otherwise meet a compulsory registration requirement, you generally do not have to register. Voluntary registration may still be an option.

Is the GST threshold based on profit or revenue?

The threshold is based on taxable turnover, not profit. Your business may have significant expenses or a low profit margin and still be required to register if its taxable supplies meet the threshold.

Can I register for GST before reaching $60,000?

Yes. Businesses can generally choose to register voluntarily if they meet the relevant requirements. However, voluntary registration also creates ongoing responsibilities, including charging GST where applicable and filing returns.

What happens if I do not register when required?

If you are required to register but fail to do so, you may become liable for GST on taxable supplies from the relevant date. Penalties and interest may also apply. You should contact Inland Revenue or an accounting professional if you think you have missed your registration obligation.

How often do I need to file GST returns?

GST returns may generally be filed monthly, two-monthly or six-monthly, depending on your business circumstances and eligibility. Your filing frequency determines how often you need to prepare and submit returns.

Can I claim GST on all business expenses?

No. GST claims are subject to eligibility rules. Generally, purchases must relate to your taxable activity and you need appropriate supporting records. Some expenses and transactions have special GST treatment.

Where can I register for GST in New Zealand?

GST registration can generally be completed through Inland Revenue's online services, myIR. You will need relevant business and identity information to complete the process.

Check the Official GST Guidance

GST rules and requirements can change. For the latest information about registration, taxable activities and filing obligations, refer to Inland Revenue's official guidance.
